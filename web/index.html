The layers

    How far to trust it

    Nine public datasets, split once into study, dev and a sealed test. The weights were written reading errors on the study split only; the sealed test was run once on the finished network. The comparisons that go against it are here too.

    Sealed test

    10,351 messages, run once. The trained model is logistic regression on hashed words, word pairs and URL parts: the classic spam filter. Given the same labels it is far ahead.

    By channel

    Sealed test, English only. On SMS the hand-written network is level with the model trained on every channel; a model trained on SMS alone (dev) reaches 99.0%.

    By source

    Sealed test. Phishing links and Enron spam are its weakest places.

    Mail of a kind never seen

    Dev split. Each source is left out in turn and the trained model learns from the other eight: most of its 97% is knowledge of these nine sources. The hand-written network is scored as it is, but its revisions read errors from every source, so for it no source is fully unseen. The clean comparison is the first draft, written before any label was read: 82.8% against 75.4%.

    Word-swap attack

    300 caught spam messages, dev split. An attacker who can query the model replaces, one at a time, the word whose swap lowers the score most with an invented word. The hand-written network keeps its evidence in fewer places: this is its main weakness.

    Its layers, with a trained output

    Dev split. The hand-written layers kept as they are and only the output layer (176 weights) trained: with 512 labels it beats a bag-of-words model with 65,875 weights. The layers are a good representation; the hand-set output weights are where most is lost.

    How it's made

    A neural network whose every weight was typed by hand with its reason: no training, no fitting, every decision traceable. A fun experiment, not a production filter.

    How the weights were written

    A first draft came from general knowledge alone, before any label was looked at. Each revision read the network's errors on the study split and added a concept the errors showed missing: receipts against payment requests, news prose, word salad, phishing kits on hacked sites, friends who refer to money they already agreed on. Never a dataset's names or addresses.

    Then the network grew: sentence and message layers, a character layer of stem filters, a letter-pair layer, 36 attention heads in two layers and a three-layer MLP, to 25,114 non-zero weights. Fourteen times the weights bought less than a point. A hand-written weight is a fact someone has to know and write down; size is not what such a network lacks.

    How the page works

    The page loads the weights (model/spam.json, 110 KB) and the engine (spam.js) and runs the network in your browser on every keystroke. Nothing is sent anywhere.

    The verdict is the network's output: a logistic of the weighted evidence, spam at 50% or more. The percentage is a score, not a calibrated probability.
